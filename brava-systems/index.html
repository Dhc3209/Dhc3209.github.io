Brava Systems

Composite profiles that look like the real thing

Brava makes polymer composite roof tiles molded into three classic looks — Old World Slate, Spanish Barrel Tile, and Cedar Shake. They share Class 4 impact resistance (UL 2218), fire ratings of Class C standard or Class A over qualifying underlayment, and a manufacturer 50-year limited warranty (terms, registration, and transfer rules apply). At CPR, Brava is a secondary option when the home calls for a slate, clay-tile, or shake aesthetic — GAF Master Elite systems remain our primary recommendation for most Lake Norman roofs.

Why homeowners ask about Brava

  • Natural slate / clay / cedar appearance without the weight or upkeep of the originals
  • Class 4 impact rating — the top UL 2218 hail category where packages qualify
  • Colorcast / mineral-infusion color that holds outdoors (confirm samples in person)
  • High recycled polymer content; recyclable at end of life per manufacturer
  • Strong wind performance when fastened to Brava’s published schedules

Product claims follow Brava’s published materials. Warranty, fire, and wind details depend on the exact profile, underlayment, and fastening — we’ll confirm at your free inspection. Official product info: bravarooftile.com · roof tiles overview.

Performance snapshot

What Brava brings to Carolina weather

Modest summary from Brava’s published specs — we’ll match profile, underlayment, and fastening to your roof at inspection.

C4

Class 4 impact

All three Brava profiles carry UL 2218 Class 4 impact resistance — the highest class in that test. Ask your insurer whether a Class 4 roof qualifies for any premium credit.

FR

Fire ratings

Class C with standard underlayment; Class A when installed over a qualifying fire-resistant underlayment assembly (per Brava testing).

50

50-year limited warranty

Manufacturer limited warranty on qualifying installs — registration, transfer, and claim limits apply. Workmanship coverage is separate; CPR stands behind the install.

WT

Lighter than natural

Composite polymer is far lighter than quarried slate or clay barrel tile — often easier on older framing while still delivering deep profile and shadow lines.
