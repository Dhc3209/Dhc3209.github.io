Storm damage & insurance

Hail, wind, and storm damage — documented the right way

After a storm, you need clear facts — not pressure. Campbells Precision Roofing provides a free inspection, documents what we find, and works the claim with your adjuster alongside you. You stay in the conversation with your insurance company.

Compliant promise: We document storm damage and support the claim process with you and your adjuster. We do not waive deductibles. We do not “handle the claim so you don’t talk to insurance.”

First 48 hours

  • Make the property safe — avoid walking a damaged roof yourself.
  • Photograph visible debris, leaks, and interior stains from the ground when safe.
  • Call or text (704) 280-5996 for a free storm inspection.
  • Notify your insurer that you’ve scheduled a professional inspection — keep your claim number handy.
  • Temporary tarp/dry-in may be recommended to limit further damage; we’ll explain options at inspection.

Inspect and document

We inspect the roof system (covering, flashing, vents, penetrations, and related components) and document findings with photos and notes suitable for an insurance conversation. You’ll get a clear explanation of what we saw — storm-related damage versus normal wear — in plain language.

Meet the adjuster

When your insurer schedules an adjuster visit, we can meet on-site to walk the roof, review documentation, and discuss scope. You remain the policyholder; we support you with technical detail so the claim discussion is informed and accurate.

Matching and discontinued shingles

Storm damage often affects only part of a roof. Matching older shingles can be difficult when colors have aged or products have been discontinued. We’ll discuss repair versus partial or full replacement options honestly — including aesthetic match limitations — so you can decide with your adjuster what the policy and the home need.

Two-layer tear-off (NC Residential Code R908)

North Carolina Residential Code section R908 addresses reroofing. In many cases, when a roof already has two layers of roof covering, a full tear-off is required before a new roof is installed. We’ll verify existing layers at inspection and explain how code applies to your specific home — not as legal advice, but as practical guidance from a licensed contractor (NC Residential license #101889).

Storm damage vs. code upgrades

Insurance typically addresses sudden storm damage. Bringing decks, ventilation, ice/water protection, or other details up to current code may be a separate conversation — sometimes covered as code-upgrade provisions, sometimes a homeowner choice. We separate storm scope from recommended upgrades so you and your adjuster can see what’s what.
